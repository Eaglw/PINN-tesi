\chapter{Introduction}
\label{chap:introduction}

Polymeric solutions, melts, and biological suspensions exhibit viscoelastic behavior
where viscous dissipation coexists with elastic memory \cite{bird1987dynamics, macosko1994rheology}.
Unlike Newtonian fluids, where the stress responds instantaneously
to the rate of deformation, polymeric media possess an internal macromolecular structure.
Under flow, dissolved polymer chains stretch and relax, storing elastic energy
along streamlines.
As a result, the local stress state depends on the entire deformation history
experienced by the fluid elements.
Accurately predicting these coupled velocity and latent stress fields
under mixed shear and extensional kinematics is essential for both process engineering
and rheological characterization.

% 1.1 BACKGROUND AND MOTIVATION

\section{Background and Motivation}
\label{sec:background_motivation}

Among benchmark rheometric systems, the four-roll mill, introduced by Taylor \cite{taylor1934formation}
and later characterized by Fuller, Leal, and Bentley \cite{fuller1980flow, fuller1981flow, bentley1986experimental},
generates two-dimensional linear flows far from solid walls.
By independently adjusting the angular speed and direction of four cylindrical rollers
disposed symmetrically in a closed square cavity, the device can produce different flow regimes,
from pure rotation to simple shear and planar elongation.

Fluid elements near this point experience prolonged extensional deformation
and long residence times.
When processing viscoelastic fluids in this regime, polymer chains stretch strongly
along the outflow symmetry axis, while undergoing shear within the narrow gaps
between adjacent rollers \cite{davino2017numerical}.
The combination of strong extensional and shearing flows within a closed cavity
with zero net mass throughput makes the four-roll mill a demanding benchmark
for both experimental rheology and numerical simulation.

% 1.2 STATE OF THE ART AND RESEARCH GAP
% ============================================================
\section{State of the Art and Research Gap}
\label{sec:state_of_the_art}

Numerical simulation of viscoelastic flows conventionally relies on mesh-based discretization,
such as the Finite Element Method (FEM) and the Finite Volume Method (FVM) \cite{owens2002computational}.
These solvers lose stability as elasticity increases, a known difficulty called
\textit{High-Weissenberg Number Problem} (HWNP).
Near extensional stagnation points, the Oldroyd-B model \cite{oldroyd1950formulation}
develops an unphysical stress singularity above a critical extension rate.
Non-linear constitutive models, such as the Giesekus \cite{giesekus1982simple}
and Phan-Thien--Tanner (PTT) \cite{phanthien1977new} equations, mitigate this divergence
but produce steep, localized stress boundary layers.
Resolving these gradients on a computational grids requires fine resolution
and specialized numerical stabilization.

Experimentally, optical techniques such as Particle Image Velocimetry (PIV)
provide planar velocity fields $\boldsymbol{u}(x, y)$.
However, non-intrusive measurement of the internal polymeric stress tensor $\boldsymbol{\tau}(x, y)$
and pressure $p(x, y)$ in closed geometries remains difficult.
Finding unknown constitutive parameters (including the relaxation time $\lambda$,
polymeric viscosity $\mu_p$, mobility $\alpha$, or extensibility $\varepsilon$)
directly from velocity measurements requires solving an inverse problem
that numerical solvers are not structured to address.

Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) \cite{raissi2019physics, cuomo2022scientific}
are a step forward respect to standard neural network and
address this limitation by embedding conservation equations and constitutive laws
directly into the loss function through automatic differentiation \cite{baydin2018automatic}.
This formulation allows the simultaneous reconstruction of unmeasured fields
and the estimation of physical parameters.
Thakur, Raissi, and Ardekani \cite{thakur2024viscoelasticnet} demonstrated this approach
with the \textit{ViscoelasticNet} framework, using velocity measurements
to recover polymeric stresses and constitutive parameters in two benchmark geometry.

Most physics-informed studies in rheology have focused on open geometries with net flow
and prescribed pressure drops, such as contractions, channels, or cross-slots.
Applying this methodology to a closed, recirculating cavity with zero net flux
raises two main challenges:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Maintaining optimization stability when polymeric stresses advect along closed streamlines
    through alternating shear and extensional regions;
    \item Determining whether parameter identification remains well posed when supervision
    is limited to planar kinematics and roller boundary conditions.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Objectives and Methodology}
\label{sec:objectives_methodology}

This thesis develops and evaluates a Physics-Informed Neural Network framework
for field reconstruction and inverse rheological characterization of steady,
two-dimensional viscoelastic flows in the four-roll mill.

The network architecture uses three decoupled Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs)
with smooth Sigmoid Linear Unit (SiLU) activations.
Mass conservation ($\nabla \cdot \boldsymbol{u} \equiv 0$) is satisfied analytically
by defining velocities through a scalar stream function $\psi(x, y)$,
while the pressure $p(x, y)$ is anchored through a single-point gauge condition
evaluated during the forward pass.
To handle the stiffness disparity between the momentum and constitutive equations \cite{wang2021understanding},
we use a two-stage training strategy paired with a mixed-precision schedule:
an initial exploration stage using Adam in single precision (FP32) \cite{kingma2014adam},
followed by refinement using L-BFGS in double precision (FP64) \cite{nocedal1980updating, liu1989limited}.
In addition, transfer learning is used to accelerate convergence.

The framework is tested against fine-grid reference solutions (up to 125{,}456 degrees of freedom)
generated in COMSOL Multiphysics \cite{comsol_multiphysics}.
The investigation addresses three main topics:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Direct field reconstruction}: Reconstructing the latent extra-stress components
    ($\tau_{xx}, \tau_{xy}, \tau_{yy}$) and pressure $p$ across the cavity
    from velocity observations and known fluid properties.
    \item \textbf{Inverse parameter estimation}: Determining constitutive parameters
    in linear (Oldroyd-B) and non-linear shear-thinning (Giesekus) fluids
    across varying elasticity and collocation densities, supported by stress anchoring
    on the roller boundaries.
    \item \textbf{Identifiability limits and physical invariances}: Characterizing the boundaries
    of inverse rheology in recirculating flows, including the structural invariance
    of the elastic shear modulus $G = \mu_p / \lambda$, hydrodynamic scaling invariance
    in creeping flow ($Re \ll 1$), and parameter correlation in linear PTT fluids.
\end{itemize}
