    \vspace*{1.2cm}
    {\Large \textbf{Abstract}}
    \vspace{0.8cm}
\end{center}

\noindent

The characterization of viscoelastic fluids presents significant challenges in both industrial processing and fundamental rheology.
Accurately modeling non-Newtonian behavior requires non-trivial governing
equations coupling momentum conservation with frame-indifferent constitutive
laws. To investigate this rheological complexity, the four-roll
mill provides a benchmark geometry: by modulating the relative rotation speeds
of four cylinders ($\Omega$ and $-k\Omega$, with $k \in [-1, 1]$), the apparatus
generates pure rotation, simple shear, pure planar elongation, and continuous
mixed kinematic regimes in between, providing an ideal platform to probe
viscoelastic response in these non-trivial flow regimes.

Characterizing viscoelastic properties from experimental velocity data
(such as Particle Image Velocimetry) constitutes an inverse problem that standard
Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) is not well suited to solve. In fact, recovering unknown
constitutive parameters via mesh-based solvers requires an expensive, iterative
trial-and-error procedure involving repeated simulations.

This thesis addresses these challenges by developing a Physics-Informed Neural
Network (PINN) framework specifically tailored to this geometry. % for the four-roll mill—a benchmark geometry capable of generating diverse shear and extensional flow regimes.
By embedding the governing conservation laws and frame-indifferent constitutive
equations directly into the neural network's loss objective, the proposed
framework enables the simultaneous reconstruction of unmeasured latent fields,
such as polymeric stress and hydrodynamic pressure, and the identification of
unknown rheological parameters. The framework is evaluated through both forward
field reconstruction and inverse parameter discovery tasks across a variety of
constitutive models, including Oldroyd-B and Giesekus fluids. Beyond parameter
identification, inferring non-linear properties such as the Giesekus mobility
factor enables data-driven constitutive model selection, discerning which
mathematical formulation best represents the experimental fluid response. The
results demonstrate the capability of the PINN solver to act as a ``virtual rheometer’’, accurately recovering physical states and parameters in complex,
recirculating flows. Finally, this work maps the fundamental limits of the
method, characterizing the physical invariances and identifiability boundaries
that govern the successful inversion of viscoelastic properties in closed
kinematic systems.
