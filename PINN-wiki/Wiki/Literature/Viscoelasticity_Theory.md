# Literature: Viscoelasticity Theory (Wikipedia)

## Summary
Viscoelasticity is a material property combining viscous and elastic characteristics. It is common in polymers where macromolecules form temporary entanglements.

## Key Methodology
- **Linear Models**:
    - **Maxwell Model**: Spring and dashpot in series ($\sigma + \frac{\eta}{E}\dot{\sigma} = \eta\dot{\epsilon}$). Good for relaxation, poor for creep.
    - **Kelvin-Voigt Model**: Spring and dashpot in parallel ($\sigma = E\epsilon + \eta\dot{\epsilon}$). Good for creep, poor for relaxation.
    - **Standard Linear Solid (Zener)**: Three-element model describing both creep and relaxation properly.
    - **Burgers Model**: Four-element model incorporating viscous flow.
- **Nonlinear Models**:
    - **Upper-convected Maxwell (UCM)**.
    - **Oldroyd-B**.
    - **Wagner Model**.
- **Dimensionless Numbers**:
    - **Deborah Number (De)**: Ratio of relaxation time to experimental time scale ($De = \lambda / t$).

## Key Findings
- **Properties**: Hysteresis in stress-strain curves, stress relaxation (constant strain), and creep (constant stress).
- **Temperature Effects**: Highly dependent on temperature (Glass phase, Rubber phase, Melt phase).
- **Measurement**: Shear and extensional rheometry.
