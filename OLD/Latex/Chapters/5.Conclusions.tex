\chapter{Conclusions and Future Perspectives}
\label{chap:conclusions}

This thesis evaluated a Physics-Informed Neural Network (PINN) framework for the
modeling and inverse rheological characterization of steady, two-dimensional
viscoelastic flows in a four-roll mill geometry. By imposing conservation of mass,
momentum balance, and constitutive laws into a decoupled multi-head
architecture, we investigated continuous latent field reconstruction and inverse
parameter estimation across three constitutive models with increasing
nonlinearity: Oldroyd-B, Giesekus, and linear Phan-Thien-Tanner (PTT) fluids.

To capture velocity, pressure, and stress in a closed recirculating cavity, we
used a three-head architecture ($\mathcal{N}_\psi, \mathcal{N}_p,
\mathcal{N}_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}$) with $\mathrm{SiLU}$ activations and zero output
initialization for stress and pressure. Formulating the velocity through a stream
function $\psi(x, y)$ enforces incompressibility ($\nabla \cdot \boldsymbol{u}
\equiv 0$) by construction, while pressure gauge indeterminacy is fixed by an
algebraic single-point anchor. Training is divided into two stages: Phase~1 optimizes
kinematics and viscoelastic stresses, while Phase~2 optimizes momentum balance
and pressure while keeping the stream function trainable. Combining FP32 Adam
for initial exploration with FP64 L-BFGS for higher-order derivatives provided
stable convergence without optimization plateaus.

In direct mode on the canonical Oldroyd-B benchmark ($Wi = 0.0833, Re = 0.0417$),
velocity supervision alone allowed the network to reconstruct extra-stresses and
pressure across the domain. Compared to the COMSOL reference solution on 125,456
nodes, relative $L_2$ errors were $0.98\%$ for $u$ and $0.93\%$ for $v$. Extra-stress
errors were $1.21\%$ for $\tau_{xy}$, $1.80\%$ for $\tau_{xx}$, and $1.65\%$ for
$\tau_{yy}$, resolving localized stress peaks in roller gaps. The pressure field
matched reference profiles with an $8.13\%$ range-relative error after gauge
calibration.

In inverse mode, the network identified fluid parameters using planar velocity
data and roller boundary stress anchoring, without interior stress measurements.
For Oldroyd-B fluids, relaxation time $\lambda$ and polymeric viscosity $\mu_p$
were identified within $0.41\%\text{--}1.27\%$ error across low and moderate
elasticity regimes ($Wi = 0.08$ and $1.17$). For shear-thinning Giesekus fluids,
the solver estimated three parameters ($\alpha, \lambda, \mu_p$) simultaneously,
recovering mobility $\alpha$ within $5.76\%\text{--}6.70\%$ error even up to the
theoretical limit $\alpha = 0.50$. Transfer learning cut training iterations from
50,000 to 25,000 steps and stabilized convergence at higher Weissenberg numbers,
while parameter estimates remained accurate down to 5,086 collocation points.

Alongside these predictive achievements, several physical and operational
limitations of the framework must be addressed. First, the inverse solver still
relies on stress data prescribed along the rotating cylinder boundaries
($\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{roll\_stress}}$) to break the scaling ambiguity with
polymeric viscosity $\mu_p$; developing strategies to infer the stress scale
without requiring surface traction or optical measurements remains an open
challenge. Second, in the creeping flow regime investigated ($Re \ll 1$), the
velocity field is kinematically dominated by roller rotation and insensitive to
the solvent viscosity $\eta_s$. Because variations in $\eta_s$ merely shift the
pressure gradient $\nabla p$, identifying the solvent contribution from velocity
data alone stagnates, indicating that higher-inertia regimes or independent
pressure measurements must be explored. Third, the identifiability of linear PTT
fluids remains problematic in this geometry, as the concentration of velocity data
in shear zones leaves the extensional parameter $\varepsilon$ weakly observable.

These physical and operational findings highlight several practical extensions
for future research:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Changing flow field parameter}:
    The present benchmark relies on four identical counter-rotating cylinders,
    which enforce purely extensional flow at the cavity center. Future work
    should evaluate asymmetric or time-dependent boundary kinematics, such as
    independent cylinder rotation speeds or alternating drive ratios (varying
    the kinematic parameter $k$). Testing these configurations will verify
    whether the decoupled architecture adapts to mixed shearing, rotational, and
    unsteady dynamics away from strict spatial symmetry.

    \item \textbf{Addition of Gaussian Noise}:
    The synthetic datasets used throughout this work represent ideal, noise-free
    velocity fields. In physical Particle Image Velocimetry (PIV), observations
    are inevitably affected by optical noise and tracer tracking uncertainties.
    Adding controlled Gaussian noise to planar velocity and roller boundary
    observations will establish the sensitivity of automatic differentiation
    under realistic experimental conditions, determining the signal-to-noise
    threshold below which explicit smoothing filters or loss regularization are
    required.

    \item \textbf{Exponential Constitutive PTT}:
    The current study implemented linear PTT fluids, in which the stress trace
    enters linearly as $1 + (\varepsilon \lambda / \mu_p)\mathrm{tr}(\boldsymbol{\tau})$.
    Extending the formulation to the exponential PTT (ePTT) model, where
    $Y(\mathrm{tr}(\boldsymbol{\tau})) = \exp[(\varepsilon \lambda / \mu_p)\mathrm{tr}(\boldsymbol{\tau})]$,
    introduces steeper non-linear gradients during backpropagation. Investigating
    ePTT will test whether the staged optimization scheme remains stable when
    confronted with exponential stress growth near stagnation lines.

    \item \textbf{Convergence Mapping Across Parameter Space}:
    Systematic parameter sweeps across Weissenberg numbers ($Wi$), Reynolds
    numbers ($Re$), and loss weights are needed to map the complete
    convergence of the solver. This research will identify the precise
    boundaries between smooth parameter recovery and numerical stiffness,
    defining the operational window where this physics-informed framework is
    most reliable.
\end{enumerate}

Physics-informed neural networks offer a practical framework to combine
kinematic measurements with constitutive modeling in viscoelastic flows. By
embedding conservation laws and objective stress transport into decoupled
network branches, the model reconstructs unobserved stress and pressure fields
directly from planar velocity data. The ability to use PINNs as a virtual
rheometer suggests that high-speed, non-intrusive characterization of polymer
melts is possible using only surface-based velocity measurements (PIV),
potentially reducing the cost of quality control in extrusion processes. By
resolving localized boundary stresses, reproducing central extensional features,
and estimating constitutive parameters without a numerical mesh, the framework
provides a foundation for data-driven rheological diagnostics in polymer
processing.
